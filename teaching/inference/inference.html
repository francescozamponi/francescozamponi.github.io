Objectives of the course:

  • Provide the foundations of statistical inference: probability, information theory, Bayesian and maximum-entropy methods, and their connections to classical statistical mechanics
  • Introduce modern high-dimensional inference and machine-learning methods: dimensionality reduction, regularization, network inference, supervised and unsupervised learning
  • Apply these methods to real biological data (protein sequences and structures, neural activity, genomic sequences) through hands-on computational tutorials

Prerequisites:

 The prerequisites are the basic mathematics and probability covered in any bachelor's degree in physics, mathematics or computer science. In particular, a working knowledge of the following is essential: the basics of mathematical analysis (limits, derivatives, integrals), linear algebra (quadratic forms, eigenvalue problems, change of basis and diagonalization of matrices), and probability. Because the course includes hands-on computational tutorials, basic programming skills (e.g. Python) are useful but not required.
